# `_learnit`: didactic references

Sources behind the "Mentor style" section in `skills/learnit/SKILL.md`. Collected 2026-11-21 by web search. For some sources I know only the abstracts, not the full text. Where research results are mixed, I say so plainly.

| Practice in the skill | Source | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship, empathy, belief in the learner; guiding questions instead of lecturing; error → hint first; reflection after success | Lepper & Woolverton (2002), the INSPIRE model of expert tutors: [UBC INSPIRE Guidelines](https://www.eoas.ubc.ca/research/cwsei/resources/INSPIRE-Guidelines.pdf), [summary](https://stafforini.com/works/lepper-2002-wisdom-practice-lessons/) | Effective tutors care about both the learner's cognitive progress and their motivation and emotions. They act warmly (Nurturant), through questions (Socratic), give indirect feedback (Indirect), ask for reflection (Reflective), and encourage (Encouraging). |
| Conversation in the second person, informal | Mayer, the personalization principle: [Cambridge, Multimedia Learning](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/multimedia-learning/personalization-voice-and-image-principles/97F9B31362E6491806A4718FECCADE3D), [NUS overview](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/medixperience/scholarly-article/mayers-12-principles-of-multimedia-instruction/) | A conversational style yields deeper learning than a formal one. The effect is strongest for beginners and when not overdone. Evidence is mixed, e.g. the [Czech replication](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/personaliz_manuscript_161226_CE_for_web.pdf) showed no advantage. |
| Feedback in three moves, about the task not the person; no empty praise | Hattie & Timperley (2007), *The Power of Feedback*: [overview](https://core.ac.uk/download/210553492.pdf), [Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/pdf) | Feedback answers three questions: where am I going, where am I, what next. The "self" level (praising the person) is the least effective. |
| Checking as low-stakes practice | Testing effect / retrieval practice: [Agarwal et al., retrievalpractice.org](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/strategies/2018/student-anxiety), [2023 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09801-w), [Liu 2024](https://metacog.bnu.edu.cn/pdf/articles/2024/liu2024.pdf) | Low-stakes retrieval strengthens memory and usually doesn't raise anxiety. High stakes discourage self-testing. |
| Choice and justification of proposals; visible progress; tie to the goal | Self-Determination Theory, Ryan & Deci (2020): [preprint](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/2020_RyanDeci_CEP_PrePrint.pdf), [meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8935530) | Intrinsic motivation is supported by autonomy, a sense of competence, and relatedness. Competence is the strongest predictor. |
| Moving away from "give information" mode towards engagement | LearnLM (Google, 2024): [arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429v3), [LearnLM-Tutor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12687) | General assistants by default hand out information instead of engaging the learner like a tutor. Pedagogy has to be spelled out to the model. |

An additional cause of the "stiff" style, found in a transcript: the user's global rule "Be extremely concise. Sacrifice grammar" produced telegraphic messages in learning. The skill takes precedence over that rule only in learning mode.
